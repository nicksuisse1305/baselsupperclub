The idea

We cook everything, and we cook it properly

Between us we have travelled to more than forty countries, and every trip left us with a dish we could not forget. So we came home and cooked it. Again and again, until it tasted like the place.

There is no single cuisine here, because there is no single cook. Nik learned in his mother's kitchen in India. Ania is a trained chef from Warsaw. Between us, most of the world's good food is covered.

One night is ramen: broth started yesterday, chilli oil made this morning, gyoza folded by hand while you watch. The next is Italian, or Thai, or an Indian night with butter chicken, lamb korma, paneer straight from the pan and naan hot from the oven. What never changes is the size of the table and how much we care about the people at it.

Nik's curries are his mother's recipes, cooked the way she taught him. Ania cooks pasta and ramen with the same love she brings to the Polish food she grew up on. Every dish has a story, and we will probably tell you.

    Your hosts

    Nik and Ania

    One of us learned to cook at home. The other went to school for it. Between the two, most of the world's good food is covered.

    We met, worked out we'd both rather plan a menu than a holiday, and started feeding people. It got out of hand in the best possible way, and now there's a table in Basel with eight seats at it.

    Hospitality is the half we care about most. Getting the food right is the entry fee. Making eight people feel properly looked after in someone else's home is the actual job.

    Nik & Ania · Basel

    India · curry, spice, the slow pots

    Nik

    Nik grew up in India in his mother's kitchen, and that is still where every curry on our table comes from — learned by watching her, by tasting constantly, and by being told when he'd got it wrong.

    He went on to study engineering at MIT, which sounds like it has nothing to do with dinner until you watch him weigh spices to the gram and keep written notes on every batch of garam masala. It's his mother's cooking, measured — and it means a dish tastes the same on the tenth evening as it did on the first.

    Add assets/gallery/hosts/ania.jpg

    Warsaw · pasta, ramen, and the Polish table

    Ania

    Ania is from Warsaw and is a certified chef — the one of us with the actual training. She cooks Italian and Japanese with equal seriousness: pasta rolled that afternoon, ramen broth started the night before, chilli oil ground by hand that morning.

    And then there's Polish food, which is where she started and the reason any of this happened. It's what she grew up eating, it's what she cooks when nobody's paying, and it turns up on our menus whenever she decides the table is ready for it.

    The small print

    Terms, privacy and imprint

    Terms of participation

    1. Booking. A request through this site is not yet a reservation. We confirm by email and hold your seat for 48 hours.
    2. Payment. Requesting a seat costs nothing and does not confirm it. We reply within 24 hours and, if there is room, send a payment link. Payment is by card, Apple Pay or Twint through Stripe; your seat is confirmed once it is paid, and the address and menu are emailed to you. We may decline a request without giving a reason. Private dining is arranged and invoiced separately.
    3. Price. CHF 120 per person for the evening’s full menu with a welcome drink, water, coffee and tea. CHF 150 per person with wine, beer, gin and tonic, vodka mate and classic cocktails poured through the evening.
    4. Cancellation. Once payment is processed it is non-refundable. You may send someone else in your place — just tell us their name in advance and we'll look after them exactly the same.
    5. Cancellation by us. An evening runs with a minimum of six guests. If we cancel or move a date, you receive a full refund or a seat at the next evening, your choice.
    6. Allergies and intolerances. Declare them when you book. We build the menu around them. We cannot accept liability for a reaction to something that wasn't declared. Ours is a home kitchen and is not free of nuts, gluten, dairy, shellfish or soy.
    7. Alcohol. Served only to guests aged 16 and over, spirits from 18, in line with Basel-Stadt rules. We reserve the right to stop pouring.
    8. The house. You are a guest in a private home. Please arrive within fifteen minutes of the stated time, don't smoke inside, and be considerate of the neighbours on the way out. Participation is at your own risk and we are not liable for personal belongings.
    9. Photographs. We sometimes photograph the food and the table. Tell us if you'd rather not appear and you won't.
    10. Law. Swiss law applies. Place of jurisdiction is Basel.
